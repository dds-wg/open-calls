The group debated strategic standardization and open infrastructure to
foster cross-community collaboration and deliberation data exchange.

The initiative aims to standardize deliberation tools through agnostic,
non-prescriptive efforts rather than replacing existing community
solutions.

**Strategic standardization and organization**\

comprehensive toolkits, ultimately prioritizing aggregation of existing
standards over strict predefined categories.

**GitHub repository aggregation strategy**\
The group decided to use the centralized repository to aggregate
existing standards, allowing for bottom-up categorization rather than
imposing rigid, top-down templates.

### **Details**

- **DDS Group Mission and Focus**: Puja Ohlhaver opened the meeting by
  questioning the core mission and function of the Decentralized
  Deliberation Stack (DDS) group, asking how the group should organize
  attention and solicit contributions ([00:00:00](#00:00:00)). Nicolas
  Gimenez clarified that DDS is not intended to replace existing groups
  but serves as an effort to standardize deliberation tools. Nicolas
  emphasized that standardization is a long-term process, and the
  group's role is to act as a pragmatic, agnostic meta-initiative that
  allows communities to conduct independent experiments and inform the
  larger group later ([00:01:15](#00:01:15)).

- **Community Outreach and Specification Challenges**: Marc-Antoine
  Parent noted the importance of inclusive design for specifications to
  ensure adoption, citing personal experience with previously published
  specs that lacked traction. Marc-Antoine suggested that the group must
  engage in outreach to connect with other communities, such as the
  Canonical Debate Lab, and warned that attempting to build a perfect,
  comprehensive schema is unrealistic ([00:04:32](#00:04:32)). Instead,
  the group should focus on federating information and building a core
  inter-reference layer that allows for evolutionary growth
  ([00:06:13](#00:06:13)).

- **Political Context and Data Exchange**: Simone Vagnoni emphasized the
  need for a protocol or meta-protocol to exchange deliberative data in
  a decentralized way, noting that democratic debates currently take
  place on private social media platforms without collective
  introspection ([00:06:13](#00:06:13)). Simone suggested that such a
  protocol could help connect different civic tech platforms, allowing
  them to communicate in a way that is suitable for their specific needs
  ([00:08:31](#00:08:31)).

- **Market Power and Strategic Standards**: Puja Ohlhaver raised
  concerns regarding the impact of market power and frontier AI
  companies on deliberation technology, suggesting that these entities
  might render open standards irrelevant if the group does not act
  strategically. Puja proposed that the group should narrow its scope to
  open standards and use collective intelligence to identify frontier
  problems, such as integrating cryptographic primitives to support
  "broad listening" without succumbing to surveillance
  ([00:10:18](#00:10:18)). The objective is to create a reactive stack
  that gains enough popularity to force frontier AI companies to
  integrate with open standards rather than overriding them
  ([00:11:37](#00:11:37)).

- **Scope of Digital Identity**: Elianna DeSota and Nicolas Gimenez
  discussed the role of digital identity within the group's scope
  ([00:14:51](#00:14:51)) ([00:23:51](#00:23:51)). Nicolas clarified
  that while digital identity is in scope as a use case, the group will
  not enforce a specific, opinionated identity wallet. Instead, the
  group will assess various decentralized identity tools based on
  objective criteria to help communities make informed decisions
  regarding their specific privacy trade-offs and needs
  ([00:15:48](#00:15:48)) ([00:24:59](#00:24:59)).

- **Strategic Spectrum and Ontology**: Marc-Antoine Parent summarized
  the discussion as a spectrum, ranging from a minimalist solution
  focusing on common use cases and base criteria to building a
  comprehensive toolkit ([00:20:18](#00:20:18)). Marc-Antoine argued for
  a middle ground that provides a common ontological denominator to
  allow for translation between diverse solutions
  ([00:21:57](#00:21:57)). Akinori Oyama added that a standardized
  approach would facilitate better benchmarking and auditing for new
  deliberation methods ([00:18:32](#00:18:32)).

- **Proposed Tracks and Categorization**: Nicolas Gimenez proposed six
  organizational tracks for the group: deliberation processes,
  incentives, ontology, infrastructure, analysis, and verification
  ([00:29:01](#00:29:01)). Puja Ohlhaver suggested that rather than
  strictly adhering to these predefined tracks, the group should
  aggregate existing standards and observe what categories naturally
  emerge ([00:30:15](#00:30:15)). Yuting Jiang introduced the
  "Participia" project, a crowdsourcing database of deliberation
  processes, suggesting it as a potential source of inspiration for
  refining the group's categorization efforts ([00:31:22](#00:31:22)).

- **Credible Neutrality and Adversarial Engagement**: Nicolas Gimenez
  reinforced the goal of establishing DDS as a credibly neutral
  initiative, hosted by the W3C, to link various deliberation fields.
  Puja Ohlhaver cautioned that credible neutrality is a complex
  concept, especially when standards are embedded within non-open
  systems, and suggested that the group should be willing to adopt an
  adversarial stance to challenge such systems and confer legitimacy

  ([00:37:09](#00:37:09)).

- **GitHub Aggregation Strategy**: Elianna DeSota proposed using the DDS

  that this aggregation is a valuable first step, helping the group

  ([00:44:41](#00:44:41)) ([00:47:53](#00:47:53)).

- **Real-World Use Cases and Expertise**: The group discussed the

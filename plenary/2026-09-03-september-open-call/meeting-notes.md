# DDS Plenary Open Call

**Date:** September 3, 2026

These reviewed notes were prepared from the Google Gemini notes and computer-generated transcript together with the Google Meet side chat. The call was not video recorded.

## Attendees

- Nicolas Gimenez
- Akinori Oyama
- Lee DeSota
- Marc-Antoine Parent
- Puja Ohlhaver
- Simone Vagnoni
- Yuting Jiang

## Summary

The group discussed how DDS can support open standards and open-source infrastructure across deliberation communities without replacing existing tools or prescribing a single stack. The immediate direction is intentionally simple: provide a shared GitHub gathering place where people can add relevant standards, tools, and use cases; invite early contributors to present their work; and learn from participation before defining a formal taxonomy or undertaking broader synthesis.

## Decisions

- **Start with a low-friction GitHub collection.** DDS should provide a shared gathering place where people can add open standards, open-source tools, evaluation criteria, and documented use cases without waiting for an elaborate submission framework.
- **Let contributors describe their own work.** Initial entries can use lightweight, self-selected tags. The group should defer a fixed taxonomy until enough material and participation exist to show which categories are useful.
- **Use calls for presentations and deliberation.** Early calls can feature a small number of contributors presenting relevant standards or implementations, with room for open discussion and cross-project learning.
- **Prioritize demonstrated use.** Initial outreach should favor work connected to real-world use cases and adoption.
- **Keep interoperability as the longer-term aim.** Connecting existing solutions through common abstractions remains important, but synthesis should follow landscape-building rather than precede it.

## Discussion

### Mission and Scope

DDS was described as a pragmatic, product- and protocol-agnostic standardization effort. Its purpose is not to replace the communities already building deliberation systems, but to help their work interoperate and converge where useful.

Marc-Antoine Parent proposed identifying a common abstraction that supports as many initiatives and shared use cases as reasonable. Simone Vagnoni emphasized the democratic need for platforms, institutions, organizations, and informal groups to exchange deliberative data securely and selectively while preserving plurality and enabling collective understanding.

### Strategic Standardization

Participants discussed the tension between building a minimal interoperability layer and producing a broader toolkit. The emerging approach was to gather existing work first, learn from concrete implementations, and avoid taking on the larger taxonomy and synthesis problem prematurely.

Puja Ohlhaver argued for a reactive open stack that can respond strategically to developments in digital identity, cryptography, AI, and other areas receiving significant investment. The group also discussed the importance of involving practitioners and experts so that any eventual standard is legitimate and informed by field experience rather than dominated by vendors or lobbyists.

### Identity and Positioning

Digital identity is in scope as an area to evaluate, but DDS should not prescribe one identity technology. The group can instead identify criteria for judging whether identity approaches protect people, preserve privacy, and meet the needs of particular deliberative use cases.

Nicolas Gimenez proposed credible neutrality as an aspiration for W3C-hosted DDS and identified the principles of interoperability, sovereignty, verifiability, resilience, usability, plurality, and fairness. Puja Ohlhaver challenged whether credible neutrality could be claimed when standards may be embedded in closed systems or shaped by market power. The more modest practical framing that emerged was DDS as a gathering place where relevant work can be presented, scrutinized, and debated.

### Tracks and Dependencies

The six proposed tracks are deliberation processes, economics/incentives/ethics, ontology, infrastructure, analysis, and verification. Participants treated a track as a subgroup, while also noting that work in the technical tracks depends on clearly described deliberation profiles and use cases.

Rather than using the tracks as mandatory submission categories, the group favored collecting existing standards and observing which categories emerge. Participedia was shared as an example of a database of participatory processes.

### Repository and Outreach

The group proposed an open call for standards and related work across the democratic deliberation stack. Contributors could add projects with lightweight, self-selected tags. A few contributors could then present their work on upcoming calls, helping DDS build participation and understand the landscape before adding more structure.

Puja Ohlhaver framed DDS as a lightweight gathering place for relevant work. Standards, tools, and use cases could be added and presented as contributors join, allowing participation, scrutiny, and discussion to shape the initiative before the group commits to a more elaborate taxonomy or synthesis process.

Possible starting points include Metagov's process-mapping work, broad-listening practice in Japan, the Canonical Debate Lab's criteria for evaluating deliberation technologies, and databases maintained by the Centre for Argument Technology.

### Post-call Clarification

In public posts after the call, Akinori Oyama clarified that Participedia's current search results for Japan do not include implementations by Liquitous, a company supporting democratic processes through digital tools and consultation. He proposed that DDS or a related stack could help evaluate AI-and-democracy processes, including proprietary or private implementations, so that their properties and trade-offs can be compared. This was a post-call proposal, not a decision made during the meeting.

## Next Steps

- Create a simple area in the DDS GitHub organization where contributors can add standards, open-source tools, evaluation frameworks, and use cases.
- Publish minimal contribution instructions and allow submitters to suggest their own tags; do not block initial contributions on a comprehensive template or taxonomy.
- Seed the collection with resources already shared by participants, prioritizing work with demonstrated use and adoption.
- Invite a small number of standards authors and practitioners to present relevant work on upcoming calls.
- Marc-Antoine Parent will contribute the Canonical Debate Lab's feature and evaluation criteria.
- Akinori Oyama will share a resource on broad-listening practices in Japan.
- Nicolas Gimenez will circulate a summary and proposed path forward to the group.
- The chairs will coordinate, asynchronously if convenient, to prepare the next open-call agenda; Simone Vagnoni offered to facilitate the next session.
- Use future monthly open calls to combine presentations of relevant standards with community deliberation, adjusting the format as participation grows.

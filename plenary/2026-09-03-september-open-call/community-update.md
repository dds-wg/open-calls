# September DDS Call: Community Update

At the September 3 DDS plenary, we discussed what the group should produce and how to begin without imposing a monolithic stack or a premature taxonomy on the deliberation ecosystem.

## Attendees

- Nicolas Gimenez
- Akinori Oyama
- Lee DeSota
- Marc-Antoine Parent
- Puja Ohlhaver
- Simone Vagnoni
- Yuting Jiang

## What We Agreed

- Start simply by making the DDS GitHub organization a shared gathering place where people can add relevant open standards, open-source tools, evaluation criteria, and real-world use cases.
- Keep initial contributions lightweight. Contributors can describe and tag their own work rather than fitting it into a comprehensive predefined schema.
- Invite a small number of contributors to present relevant work on upcoming calls, followed by open deliberation and comparison across approaches.
- Prioritize projects connected to real implementations, use cases, and adoption.
- Revisit categorization and interoperability synthesis after the collection and contributor community have grown enough to inform that work.

The longer-term ambition remains to help independently developed deliberation systems connect through shared abstractions while respecting different community needs and trade-offs. We also discussed digital identity, privacy-preserving broad listening, credible neutrality, and the importance of evaluating technologies against transparent criteria rather than prescribing one universal solution.

## Contributions and Starting Points

- Nicolas Gimenez emphasized that DDS is a long-term W3C standardization effort, not a replacement for existing communities or a vehicle for prescribing one product or protocol. Work should begin with clearly defined deliberation profiles and use cases, which can then inform the ontology, infrastructure, analysis, and verification tracks. Digital identity is in scope, but DDS should evaluate available approaches against transparent criteria and use-case-specific trade-offs rather than mandate one solution. The effort should uphold interoperability, sovereignty, verifiability, resilience, usability, plurality, and fairness.
- Puja Ohlhaver challenged whether DDS could claim credible neutrality, noting that standards can remain shaped by closed systems and market power. She proposed the more modest framing of DDS as a lightweight gathering place for relevant standards, tools, and use cases. Contributors could add and present work as they join, allowing participation, scrutiny, and discussion to shape how DDS evolves.
- Elianna DeSota described Metagov's work to document and diagram deliberation processes. That work is still being prepared for publication with the agreement of participating communities, but it could become an important early contribution when available.
- Akinori Oyama proposed exploring a practical toolbox, privacy-preserving audits, and secret-execution or computation environments. He also offered to share resources and connections related to broad-listening practices in Japan.
- Marc-Antoine Parent shared the Canonical Debate Lab's existing feature and evaluation criteria, along with an earlier collection of deliberation technologies. He emphasized working in both directions: from shared abstractions toward implementations and from concrete processes toward common properties.
- Yuting Jiang shared Participedia as an existing database of participatory processes and proposed using open calls to combine presentations of relevant standards with community deliberation.
- Simone Vagnoni emphasized the need for secure, selective exchange of deliberative data across platforms, institutions, and communities without losing complexity or plurality.

## Post-call Follow-up

After checking the available records, Akinori clarified that [Participedia's current results for Japan](https://participedia.net/search?selectedCategory=all&query=japan&layout=null) do not include implementations by [Liquitous](https://liquitous.com/en), a company that supports democratic processes through digital tools and consultation. Its [Japanese website](https://liquitous.com) provides more detailed information.

Akinori also proposed that the landscape should support evaluations of AI-and-democracy processes even when the underlying implementations are proprietary or private. A stack or evaluation framework such as DDS could make their properties and trade-offs easier to compare without treating inclusion as endorsement. See Akinori's posts about [Liquitous](https://x.com/Aki_laid_back/status/2095707857937391740) and the [Participedia gap and evaluation proposal](https://x.com/Aki_laid_back/status/2095721079658655785).

## Next Steps

1. Create a simple GitHub contribution area with minimal instructions.
2. Seed it with standards, tools, criteria, and use cases already identified by participants.
3. Invite the first contributors to add and present their work.
4. The chairs will coordinate, asynchronously if convenient, to prepare the next open-call agenda and a recurring format that combines presentations of relevant standards with community deliberation.
5. Share progress and invitations through the DDS community channels.
